# Decisions

Choices made where the brief left room, or where building it showed a detail the brief did not settle.

| Question | Choice | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Three-direction design lab? | Skipped; one direction ("Plywood") built directly | Owner's budget instruction during the design build. Rejected alternatives are in DESIGN.md. |
| Light or dark? | Both: follows the system, with an Appearance setting (Auto / Light / Dark). The training room is dark in both | Owner asked for a dark mode; this overrides the brief's "no user-selectable themes in v1". |
| Counting ~100M games in 32 GB | Lossy counting with a 300k-game bucket per band; positions as 64-bit Zobrist keys | Exact counts do not fit. Error is at most ≈10 games per count at 3M games, well under the 50-game cut. |
| Stockfish for eval gaps | 10 single-threaded Stockfish 17 processes, depth 18, 64 MB hash each | Same total compute as the brief's "10 threads, 1 GB", but more positions per second. Most-reached positions run first. |
| Moves listed under a tree node | Moves with ≥ 10 games | Keeps rarely played moves visible in Explore without blowing the size budget. Their child positions may be below the 50-game cut. |
| Correspondence games | Kept (TimeControl "-" counts as long) | The only filter in the brief is "no bullet". They are a tiny share. |
| Punish answer length | Engine line cut at the first quiet move of the punishing side (captures and checks only after the first move), up to 3 moves | A quiet follow-up has many good moves; requiring the engine's exact choice would grade a fine answer as wrong. |
| Danger answer | Your repertoire move at that position (default move if you have none), never the bait | Your repertoire may differ from the default move; the card must agree with your lines. |
| A trap in the tree but not in your lines | Not added as a card; shown in Explore with a preview | Brief: only traps whose position appears in your lines are added. |
| New Move card "enters learning step 1" | First FSRS rating is Again (1-minute step), logged with stage "new" | ts-fsrs puts a new card rated Again on the first learning step. Intro reviews are excluded from retention stats. |
| Line coverage when the tree runs out | Counts as covered | Lines stop where no reply has ≥ 50 games; the games there have not left your lines. Replies below the threshold where others pass it count as uncovered. |
| Openings in Repertoire and Stats | Grouped by opening family of each position (Italian Game, Sicilian Defense, …), per section | Lichess names change along a line; grouping by family gives stable, recognisable rows. Positions before any name are "First moves". |
| "Also in N other lines" | Other move orders found while walking your lines (different parent position or move) | Lines are generated, so the transpositions inside them are known exactly. |
| Learn rounds | 5 new moves shown Learn style, then quizzed in their lines; missed ones go to the front of the next round | As the brief, with "repeat at the end of the round" done by carrying them into the next round. |
| Branch cue (replaces the brief's branch label) | Where you have two moves in one position, Birch says which one is asked with the plainest difference between them (`lib/cue.ts`): what the move does on the board (castle, take, check, which piece, which pawn), then main move or sideline by frequency, then the opening name, then the move itself. Shown only at that position, not from move 1 | Opening names cannot be memorised as cues, and a cue such as "take the pawn" shown several moves early would read as an instruction for the wrong position. The cue depends only on the position, your moves and the data, so it is the same every time. Mood words (aggressive, solid) are not used: they cannot be derived reliably from the data. |
| Line colour in the trainer | The line's colour from the skill tree lights the session room from above (brighter when the line is done), sits on its name as a rail and a tint, fills its moves as tinted chips from where it branches off, and ends on a solid glowing chip. Moves on the trunk, before the opening's first split, have no colour. The board's right-answer mark stays green | A ring and a fading outline on the board were too faint to notice. The colour belongs to the line, so it sits on the line and around it. The eight colours were recalibrated to one lightness and chroma so none is louder than another. |
| Skill tree layout | A move's answers either stack below one another or stand side by side on the row under it; each part of the tree is given a shape to aim for (the whole tree about 1.6 wide to 1 high) and takes whichever arrangement comes closer. Tools are only zoom out, zoom in and Expand all / Collapse, top right on a phone | Stacking every answer made a large opening many times taller than wide (the fully unfolded Italian was about one screen wide and twenty high). Expand all goes to a readable zoom from the first move instead of fitting 168 tiles on a phone; `0` still fits the whole tree. Next up, Fit, Fold and the Key were removed on request. |
| Eval bar while you are asked | In the trainer it stays blank (in place, so the board does not resize) until the line is answered. In Play it out it is empty until your first move and changes only after you have answered | Updated on the opponent's move, a swing told you which line this is or that there was something to find. |
| Drill when you branch | Any of your moves at a branch is accepted; the rest of the drill is drawn again from the branch you played | Brief: "the drill continues down the branch you picked". |
| Due learning cards in a session | When the queue ends, cards in a learning step due within 10 minutes are played again | Brief: cards due again within the session are re-queued while the app is open. |
| On-device engine | Stockfish 19 lite, single-threaded WASM (stockfish.js npm package, GPL-3.0) | The brief asks for "Stockfish lite WASM (single-threaded)"; 19 is the current build of that package. 1.8 MB, no cross-origin isolation needed. |
| Mistake eval after your move | From the MultiPV line when your move is in the top 3; otherwise a 150 ms search of the position after it | Keeps analysis near the brief's 12 s per game. |
| Positions for the move 10 / 15 chart | Plies 20 and 30 always get an eval | They may be the opponent's turn, which the per-move analysis skips. |
| Opening data cache | IndexedDB only; the service worker precaches the app shell and engine, not `data/` | Avoids storing the data twice on the phone. Offline after first launch either way. |
| Data update on mobile data | Only on Wi-Fi when the browser reports the connection type; otherwise when the app next opens on Wi-Fi | Brief: changed files download on Wi-Fi. Browsers that do not report it are treated as Wi-Fi. |
| First chess.com rating | Sets the band at once; only later changes wait for the 7-day hold | The hold guards against rating swings; without it on the first sync you would train the 1200 default lines for a week. |
| Promotion | Four-piece picker over the board | Brief. |
| No chess.com name at onboarding | Band 1200 with 8-move lines | Brief defaults. |
| Moves outside the band's tree | Opponent replies from an imported PGN (and from Set as my repertoire in Explore) are kept as your own book; lines follow your moves and book replies past the end of the tree and below the reply threshold, a reply the tree does not know counting as 1 % | The move editor only listed tree moves and lines stopped where the tree ended, so an imported repertoire would have been cut off silently. |
| PGN import, two moves of yours at one position | The main line's move only, unless you keep the others as branches | Studies often list alternatives for your side; a branch is a deliberate choice in Ply (brief). |
| Notes from PGN comments | Fill only empty notes; a comment after your move is that position's note, after the opponent's move the next position's | Your own notes are never overwritten; export writes notes back in the same place, so files round-trip. |
| Lichess User-Agent | Not set | Browsers cannot set it reliably, and Lichess's CORS rules do not allow the header from a page, so a browser that sends it fails the request. The Origin header names the site. |
| Lichess rating without chess.com | Lichess rapid sets the band directly (stored as rapid − 350 on the chess.com scale the depth rules use) | The brief's band is chess.com + 350, which is Lichess's scale. |
| Due count | Every due review, with "· N today" when a daily limit holds some back | Showing only the capped queue hid the backlog; catch-up needs the honest number. |
| Catch-up | Offered at 50+ due and either more than a day's limit or 3+ days away; 3, 5 or 7 days, an even share a day, new cards paused | Spreads the return without hiding cards or changing the scheduler. |
| Streak freezes | One per 7 study days in a row, at most 2, used automatically on a missed day; computed from the review log | Earned, never sold; no storage, so a backup restores them exactly. |
| Higher bands | Same rule (both players within band−100 … band+99), counted only for bands not cached, streaming into older months as needed | Keeps the existing six untouched and their cache valid. |
| FSRS weight optimisation | fsrs-rs 6.6 compiled here to single-threaded WASM (`optimizer/`), offered from 1,000 reviews, fitted in a worker, applied from the next review, Defaults to undo | The published browser build needs threads and cross-origin isolation, which GitHub Pages cannot provide; the single-threaded build needs neither. 1,000 reviews is in the range FSRS suggests before fitting. |
| Accounts: optional or required? | Required when the build has the Supabase keys: login is the first screen, progress lives on the account. Builds without keys (local, forks, CI's smoke test) run on the device only, as before | Owner's call during the sync build ("why would we have it optional?"). The keyless mode keeps forks and the smoke test working without a backend. |
| Log out | A last sync, then this device is cleared; logging in again brings everything back. If that sync fails, it says how many changes would be lost and asks first | Data lives on the account; a shared device should not keep someone's progress. |
| Delete my account | Deletes the account and every synced row (`delete_my_account()`, a security-definer function, so no service key or Edge Function), then clears this device | With login required, leaving the device's copy would upload it again into the next account. |
| Reset all progress (logged in) | Every synced row on the account becomes a deletion, so the reset reaches every device; what this device writes afterwards is stamped just after the reset | A reset on one device that the others undo by syncing would be no reset. |
| What syncs | `settings`, `repertoire`, `book`, `cards`, `reviews`, `games`; never the opening data. Device-only settings: theme, sounds, vibration, `storageNoteSeen`, `tips` | The brief's list. Device-only settings are about the device, not the player. |
| Change tracking | Dexie version 4: `updatedAt` on every synced row, set by Dexie hooks on every write (existing rows: the upgrade time). Deletions are tombstones in a separate `tomb` table, not a `deleted` flag on the row; the server tables do have `deleted` | Hooks catch every write without touching each call site. A separate tombstone table means no screen has to learn to skip deleted rows. The sync engine writes through a second connection without hooks, so pulled rows keep their own time. |
| Merge rules | Reviews: union by (card, time). Cards: the later FSRS `last_review`, then `updatedAt`. Everything else: the later `updatedAt`. A deletion compares by `updatedAt` only (beats older edits, loses to later ones). The `app` settings row merges one setting at a time by per-key stamps. A tie keeps the server's version | The brief's rules. The tie rule makes every device converge on one version. |
| Where the rules run | In the app (`lib/cloud/merge.ts`) and again in Postgres triggers that refuse an older write (`supabase/migrations`) | A device pushing stale data (a slow sync, a restored backup) cannot overwrite newer progress from another device, whatever order the pushes arrive in. |
| Pull cursor | A server-assigned sequence and time per row (`seq`, `synced_at` from `clock_timestamp()`); pulls page by `seq` and re-read the last two minutes | Client clocks differ between devices, so they cannot be the cursor. The two-minute overlap catches a write that committed after a pull although its server time was earlier; merging is idempotent, so re-reading costs nothing. |
| A new device's defaults | Rows and settings written before onboarding is finished are stamped 0, and a device that has not finished onboarding only pulls | The starter repertoire and onboarding choices on a second device must never replace an account's real repertoire. Without the pull-only rule the device's pre-onboarding defaults reached the account first and won the tie against its own onboarding choices (found by the two-device test). |
| Failed sync | The whole pull arrives before anything is written; the merge is one transaction; the cursor is saved only after the push | A failure leaves the device as it was (or, after a failed push, merged and complete); the next sync retries. |
| When it syncs | Login, opening the app (and coming back to it after 5 minutes), leaving a training session or Play it out, finishing onboarding, the connection returning, Sync now. Never during a session | The trainer holds cards in memory during a session. |
| Importing a backup while logged in | Replaces this device, then the next sync starts over and merges: rows the account has and the backup lacks come back, and a card keeps its later review | An old file must not wipe newer progress from other devices. |
| Login methods | Email and password (Supabase, with "Confirm email" on so an account needs a real address: the confirmation link logs you in; the only other email is a password reset you ask for), Lichess (OAuth with PKCE to lichess.org, then the `lichess-login` Edge Function returns a one-time Supabase login). No Google or chess.com login | No email round trip to log in, only once to confirm a new account. A reset link comes back to the app and Settings asks for the new password; on the iPhone Home Screen app the reset is done in Safari, whose storage is separate, and you then log in with the new password. Accounts made with the old magic link have no password and use "Forgot password?" once. Google login is left out for now (it needs a Google Cloud OAuth client and consent screen; Supabase's provider can be switched on later and the button brought back). chess.com's OAuth is for approved partners only. The Lichess function holds the service-role key on Supabase's side only. |
| Lichess accounts | A Ply user per Lichess id, with the address `<id>@lichess.invalid` (reserved, never receives mail) and the Lichess username in the user's metadata. The Lichess token is revoked as soon as the account is read | Supabase users need an email; Lichess gives none without an extra scope. Ply needs nothing from Lichess beyond who you are. |
| Supabase client | `@supabase/supabase-js` (MIT), loaded only in builds with keys and only when the app starts; the session is kept under `ply-auth` in localStorage | The only new runtime dependency. Keyless builds never load it. |
| Deployed build | Uses the Supabase secrets only when the repository variable `PLY_ACCOUNTS` is `true` | The live site must not demand a login before the database and auth settings exist. |
| Landing page in a build without accounts? | Such a build (local, forks, CI) still opens straight into the app; the landing page lives at `#/welcome` there (the command palette's "About Ply" and a link at the foot of Settings on a desktop) and its buttons open the app | There is no login to end in, the onboarding's first step is already a welcome, and the smoke test and every fork keep working unchanged. |
| Who sees the landing page? | Anyone not logged in, at every width, phones included; the login is its last panel and the bar's "Log in" goes straight to it | Owner's brief. A returning visitor is one tap from the form. |
| When is the opening data fetched for a visitor? | Not until they head for the login (the bar's button, a call to action, or the login panel coming near), or are logged in | A cold visit to the landing page should not download a few MB it may never use. A browser with a stored login boots as before. |
| What does the landing page's live demo run on? | `lib/landing/demo.json`, extracted from `public/data` by `npm run landing-data` and checked by a Vitest against the manifest | The real trees are megabytes per band; the page needs one line, one position at eight bands, one trap and a small tree. Every number still comes from the data. |
| Desktop breakpoint | Window size only: 1024 × 600 and up (rail to 1279, sidebar from 1280, third columns from 1600) | A device or pointer test would give an iPad on its side the phone design and break a narrow desktop window. |
| Shared-element transitions | A measured FLIP on the trainer's board (`lib/flight.ts`), not the View Transitions API; that API is used on the landing page (the band switch) where one element changes in place | Screens here leave with a Svelte fade while the next enters, so two pages share the DOM for a moment and a view transition would see duplicate names. The FLIP also works in every browser. |
| Number keys for the five screens? | No; the palette (Ctrl/Cmd+K or /) is the keyboard way around | Digits are part of typed moves (e4, Nf3) on the boards. |
| Logging out | The loading screen holds until the device is cleared and the page reloads | The landing page (and its login) would otherwise show for a moment over a device that is still being wiped. |
| A move on the line that is not due | Gets a real FSRS review either way: Again on a miss, the automatic grade on a correct answer, flagged `implicit`. No credit from a correct answer for cards in a learning step, cards with difficulty ≥ 8, or cards already reviewed today (`lib/srs/credit.ts`). Implicit reviews do not use up the review limit and are left out of retention | Logging the misses and not the successes gave the weight fit a log that looked worse than the player (reports/Chess line SRS.md). FSRS already gives an early review little gain, so no extra discount is needed; once a day keeps the moves every line shares from being credited in every line. |
| Which move order a line takes | Still the deepest due card's line, but where its position has more than one move order: the one through the most due cards, then the most nearly due (R < 0.95), then the most probable | Fewer lines for the same due cards, and nearly due moves are credited on the way. |
| Smoke test with `.env.local` keys present | Build with `VITE_SUPABASE_URL= VITE_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY= npm run build` before `npm run smoke` | A build with keys opens on the landing page and login, and the smoke test expects a build without accounts, as in CI. |
| Where a review line starts | From its landmark (the last non-main reply before the first due move, else the last branch or transposition), the trunk played fast; a trunk move that is nearly due, learning or hard is still asked; a fifth of lines a day play whole | Music and Chess Tempo practise from landmarks rather than always from the top; the whole lines keep the trunk joined up (reports/Chess line SRS.md). |
| Landmark lines for everyone? | Half the sessions at random until Settings fixes it, each review logging its arm | It changes what players see and its benefit is untested in chess, so it is measured, not assumed. |
| Extending a line past its last due move | Not built | The lines branch into every reply of 3% or more, so there is almost never a single reply to extend through; the pips show where a line ends instead. |
